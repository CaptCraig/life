Game of Life

Conway's Game of Life

The mathematician John Horton Conway invented Life in 1970. Martin Gardner described it in his Scientific American column that October, and people everywhere started tracing it out on graph paper and on early computers. It isn't really a game, because nobody plays. You set up the board and watch what happens.

The rules

Every square is alive or dead. At each tick (one generation), every square counts its eight neighbours:

  • A dead square with exactly 3 live neighbours is born.
  • A live square with 2 or 3 live neighbours survives.
  • Every other square is dead in the next generation, from loneliness or from overcrowding.

Those three rules produce gliders that crawl across the board, guns that fire them forever, and tiny seeds that churn for thousands of generations before they settle.

How to use it

  • Pick a pattern from the menu and a dim preview appears. With a mouse, the preview follows the pointer and a click drops it in. On a phone or tablet, drag the preview into position and tap ✓ Place. Rotate and Flip turn it (or R and F), and Done (or Esc) finishes.
  • Undo takes back the last thing you drew, erased or placed (up to 30 steps). Running the board starts a fresh history.
  • Scroll or pinch to zoom, and drag to move around. The board has no edges.
  • Draw lets you add or remove single squares. Erase pauses the board and gives you a round eraser: sweep it across to wipe out cells (zoom in for a small eraser, out for a big one), and Undo puts back the last sweep. Follow lets you click a glider or spaceship and ride along with it.
  • Copy: drag a box around some cells. Paste then lets you drop copies anywhere, turned or flipped, or tap Where it was to put it back exactly. Your copy is remembered on this device until you copy something else.
  • Reset takes the board back to how it was the last time you pressed Run or Step, so you can try the same start again.
  • Auto-zoom pulls back as the pattern grows, so nothing escapes the view.
  • On a keyboard: Space run or pause, N step, Z fit, +/− zoom.
